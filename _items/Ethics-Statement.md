shortdesc: "Our project, Preserving Local History at Snow Camp Outdoor Theatre, acts as an interdisciplinary study merging historical and archival practices, with a focus on historical memory and conservation. The historical materials collected from the Worthy B. Teague Museum at Snow Camp Outdoor Theatre provide insight into key Quaker theology, education, businesses, and lives of Snow Camp residents in the 1800s. Now, we may contextualize the materials with the modern community via community engagement. We seek to answer the questions of how these materials retain importance to historical memory. Alongside studying historical materials and figures, the public history component of the project highlights how the stories of the living connect to historical memory. Through conserving and contextualizing the materials and gathering oral histories of community members, we aim to gather a larger picture that tells us a historical narrative of Snow Camp and the Cane Creek Friends Meeting.
We commit to making the historical materials and oral history interviews publicly and freely accessible via physical and digital exhibits created in collaboration with Preservation Alamance and Snow Camp Community Theatre. 
	To carry out this mission and ethical framework through the oral history interview process and creation of the exhibits, we incorporated the following commitments into our work. We:
1. Contextualize the era and history in which the exhibit materials were created to better understand the people who created and engaged with them.  
2. Engage in community collaboration with the Cane Creek Friends Meeting and Snow Camp Outdoor Theatre to contextualize the Quaker roots of Snow Camp and continued modern work of the church.
3. Contextualize Quaker beliefs and practices with understanding that it is a thriving religious community that remains active in the area. 
4. Seek provenance and stories of the historical materials through oral history interviews with Snow Camp community members. 
5. When engaging in these oral history interviews, we will prioritize informed consent, collaboration, and preservation of stories. 
6. Recognize the importance of community accessibility to historical conservation and memory. To maintain accessibility, public and digital exhibits will be free and publicly accessible. 
7. Balance the need to conserve archival materials with community access and work toward building further collaborations with archival repositories that will preserve materials for future materials while ensuring materials are accessible to community members via digital scans and prints.
